## Semiconductors

Semiconductors are materials whose electrical conductivity lies between conductors and insulators. They can conduct electricity only under suitable conditions. Common examples are silicon, germanium and gallium arsenide.

In semiconductors, both electrons and holes act as charge carriers. Electrons are negatively charged carriers, while holes behave like positively charged carriers.

### Properties of Semiconductors

- Conductivity lies between conductors and insulators.
- At zero Kelvin, they behave like insulators.
- Conductivity increases with increase in temperature.

### Types of Semiconductors

#### Intrinsic Semiconductor

An intrinsic semiconductor is a pure semiconductor without any impurity. Silicon and germanium are common examples. In intrinsic semiconductors, the number of electrons and holes is equal.

#### Extrinsic Semiconductor

An extrinsic semiconductor is formed by adding a small amount of impurity to a pure semiconductor. This process is called doping.

Extrinsic semiconductors are of two types:

#### n-type Semiconductor

n-type semiconductor is formed by doping silicon or germanium with pentavalent impurities like phosphorus, arsenic or antimony. Electrons are the majority charge carriers.

#### p-type Semiconductor

p-type semiconductor is formed by doping silicon or germanium with trivalent impurities like boron, aluminium or gallium. Holes are the majority charge carriers.

### Applications

- Used in diodes and transistors
- Used in microchips
- Used in solar cells
- Used in high-speed communication devices

## Solar Cell

A solar cell or photovoltaic cell is a device that converts solar energy directly into electrical energy using a semiconductor.

### Construction

A solar cell is made using a p-n junction semiconductor. It consists of p-type and n-type silicon layers. Metal electrodes are connected to the upper and lower layers to collect current. The p-n junction is arranged in such a way that sunlight falls on it.

### Working

When sunlight falls on the p-n junction, photons transfer energy to electrons and create electron-hole pairs. Electrons move towards the n-type layer and holes move towards the p-type layer. This separation of charges produces potential difference. When an external circuit is connected, electrons flow through the circuit and produce electricity.

### Advantages

- Renewable source of energy
- Pollution-free process
- No harmful gases are released
- Can be installed easily in different sizes

## Superconductors

Superconductors are materials which show zero electrical resistance when cooled below a certain temperature called critical temperature.

The temperature at which a material changes from normal conducting state to superconducting state is called critical temperature or transition temperature.

### Properties of Superconductors

- They have zero electrical resistance below critical temperature.
- Current can flow without energy loss.
- They show perfect diamagnetism.

### Type-I Superconductors

Type-I superconductors show complete diamagnetism. They suddenly change from superconducting state to normal state when the magnetic field reaches critical value.

Examples: Aluminium, mercury, lead, tin.

### Type-II Superconductors

Type-II superconductors have two critical magnetic fields, lower critical field and upper critical field. Between these two fields, they exist in mixed state.

Examples: Niobium, vanadium, tantalum and alloys.

### Applications

- Used in MRI and NMR machines
- Used in maglev trains
- Used in low-loss power cables

## Nanomaterials

Nanomaterials are materials having at least one dimension in the range of 1 to 100 nanometers. They show different properties compared to bulk materials due to their small size and high surface area.

### Nanometals

Nanometals are metallic nanoparticles such as gold, silver and platinum nanoparticles. They have high surface area, good catalytic activity and special optical properties.

### Nano Metal Oxides

Nano metal oxides are oxide nanoparticles such as zinc oxide, titanium dioxide and iron oxide. They are useful in sensors, catalysts, cosmetics, electronics and environmental applications.

### Applications of Nanomaterials

- Used in drug delivery
- Used in sensors
- Used in water purification

## Preparation Methods

Nanomaterials can be prepared mainly by top-down and bottom-up approaches.

### Sol-Gel Method

Sol-gel method is a chemical method used to prepare nanoparticles. In this method, a liquid colloidal solution called sol is converted into a solid network called gel.

#### Process Flow

Sol → Gel → Drying → Calcination → Nanoparticle powder

### Chemical Method

Chemical precipitation is a common method for preparing nanoparticles.

#### Process Flow

Precursor solutions → Mixing → Nucleation → Growth → Filtration and washing → Drying → Nanoparticle powder

#### Example

ZnSO₄ + 2NaOH → Zn(OH)₂ + Na₂SO₄

Zn(OH)₂ → ZnO + H₂O

### Biological Method

Biological method is also called green synthesis method. It uses natural materials like plant extracts, bacteria, fungi or enzymes to prepare nanoparticles.

#### Process Flow

Metal salt → Plant extract → Reduction → Stabilization → Nanoparticles

#### Example

Ag⁺ + Plant extract → Ag nanoparticles

### Advantages of Biological Method

- Eco-friendly method
- Less toxic chemicals are used
- Low cost
- Energy efficient
- Safe and sustainable method
