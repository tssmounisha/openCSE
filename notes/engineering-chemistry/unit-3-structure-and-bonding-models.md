# Unit 3: Structure and Bonding Models

## Fundamentals of Quantum Mechanics

Quantum mechanics explains the behaviour of very small particles like electrons. Electrons show both particle and wave nature.

### Heisenberg Uncertainty Principle

It is impossible to determine the exact position and exact momentum of an electron simultaneously.

$$
\Delta x \cdot \Delta p \geq \frac{h}{4\pi}
$$

---

## Schrodinger Wave Equation

Schrodinger wave equation describes the wave nature of an electron.

$$
\frac{\partial^2 \psi}{\partial x^2}+
\frac{\partial^2 \psi}{\partial y^2}+
\frac{\partial^2 \psi}{\partial z^2}+
\frac{8\pi^2m}{h^2}(E-V)\psi=0
$$

---

## Time Independent Schrodinger Equation
Time Independent Schrodinger Equation is used to describe the wave function of a particale when potential energy is independent of time.
For one dimensional motion, the equation is:

$$
\frac{d^2 \psi}{dx^2}+
\frac{8\pi^2m}{h^2}(E-V)\psi=0
$$

---

## Significance of $\psi$ and $\psi^2$

$\psi$ is called wave function. It gives information about the wave nature of an electron.

$\psi^2$ gives the probability of finding an electron at a particular point in space.

---

## Particle in One Dimensional Box

A particle in one dimensional box is a simple model used to explain the motion of an electron inside a box.

The energy of particle is:

$$
E_n = \frac{n^2h^2}{8ma^2}
$$

Thus, energy of a particle in a box is quantized.

---

## Molecular Orbital Theory

Molecular orbital theory explains bonding in molecules by the combination of atomic orbitals.

When two atomic orbitals combine, they form bonding molecular orbital and anti-bonding molecular orbital.

Bonding molecular orbital has lower energy and increases stability.

Anti-bonding molecular orbital has higher energy and decreases stability.

---

## Calculation of Bond Order

Bond order is used to find the stability and nature of bond in a molecule.

$$
Bond\ Order = \frac{1}{2}(N_b - N_a)
$$

---

## M.O. Energy Level Diagram of Nitrogen $(N_2)$

Total number of electrons in $N_2$ = 14

Bond order:

$$
Bond\ Order = \frac{1}{2}(10-4)=3
$$

Nitrogen has triple bond and is diamagnetic.

---

## M.O. Energy Level Diagram of Oxygen $(O_2)$

Total number of electrons in $O_2$ = 16

Bond order:

$$
Bond\ Order = \frac{1}{2}(10-6)=2
$$

Oxygen has double bond and is paramagnetic.

---

## M.O. Energy Level Diagram of Carbon Monoxide $(CO)$

Total number of electrons in CO = 14

Bond order:

$$
Bond\ Order = \frac{1}{2}(10-4)=3
$$

Carbon monoxide has triple bond and is diamagnetic.

---

## π-Molecular Orbitals of Butadiene

Butadiene is a conjugated molecule with four carbon atoms and four π electrons.

Each carbon atom is $sp^2$ hybridized. The unhybridized p-orbitals overlap sideways to form π molecular orbitals.

The π electrons are delocalized over all four carbon atoms.

---

## π-Molecular Orbitals of Benzene

Benzene is a planar conjugated molecule with six carbon atoms and six π electrons.

Each carbon atom is $sp^2$ hybridized and contains one unhybridized p-orbital.

Due to delocalization of π electrons, benzene becomes more stable.
